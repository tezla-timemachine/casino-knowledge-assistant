# AML Investigation and Reporting Procedure

*Harbourlight Casino Group. Mock document for a portfolio project. Not a real company policy.*

**Restricted: Compliance team only.**

## 1. Purpose

This procedure sets out how the Compliance team handles internal suspicious activity reports (internal SARs) received from employees.

## 2. Receiving an internal SAR

Every internal SAR is logged in the Compliance case system on the day it is received and given a case number. The employee who made the report receives an acknowledgement, but no details of the outcome.

## 3. Review

A Compliance analyst reviews the customer's transaction history, play data, due diligence records and CCTV where relevant, and prepares a summary for the MLRO within 3 working days.

## 4. MLRO decision

The MLRO decides within 5 working days of receiving the internal SAR whether to submit an external Suspicious Activity Report to the National Crime Agency (NCA) through SAR Online. The reasons for the decision are recorded in every case, including when no report is made.

## 5. Defence against money laundering (DAML)

Where we need to carry out a transaction that we suspect involves criminal property, the MLRO requests a defence against money laundering from the NCA before the transaction goes ahead. The transaction must not proceed during the notice period unless consent is received.

## 6. Enhanced due diligence

Enhanced due diligence is required for politically exposed persons (PEPs), customers linked to high-risk countries, and customers whose spend increases by more than 300% within 3 months. It includes confirming the source of funds and senior management approval to continue the relationship.

## 7. Record keeping

Due diligence records and SAR records are kept for 5 years after the end of the customer relationship.

## 8. Confidentiality

Information about SARs is shared only with people who need to know. Disclosing that a SAR has been made may be a criminal offence.
